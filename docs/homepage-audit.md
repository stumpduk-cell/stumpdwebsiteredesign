# Stump’d homepage — content, UX and architecture audit

## Scope

This audit uses the owner-supplied read-only Wix snapshot covering all 36 static pages, the primary navigation, homepage copy, header/footer and installed Wix functionality. No live page or Wix app was changed.

## Executive assessment

The current homepage has the right raw ingredients but does not yet present one decisive explanation of Stump’d. It introduces pressure, practical mental-strength tools, a pathway and credible founders, but it frames the organisation mainly as “mental performance coaching for young cricketers aged 11–18”. That description is now too narrow: it omits Spark, underplays transfer into school and everyday life, and makes Stump’d sound closer to a conventional coaching service than the wider proposition in the master brief.

The page is also carrying site-architecture problems. Thirteen primary navigation links, five social links, a repeated Summer Series promotion and an inactive member login ask visitors to choose before they understand the organisation. Meanwhile the footer provides no useful recovery route. The homepage repeats pathway and planning-call actions but does not clearly route its distinct audiences.

The recommended response is not to add more content. It is to establish a sharper proposition, correct the brand framework and programme facts, reduce the global navigation, introduce clear audience routes, and let the homepage act as the front door to a larger mental-strength-through-sport organisation.

## What currently works

### 1. The core strapline is immediate

“Think Well. Play Better.” is memorable and appropriately prominent. It should remain the H1 and should not be rewritten.

### 2. The problem section begins with recognisable moments

Mistakes, falling confidence, emotions, pressure and selection are concrete experiences. This is stronger than generic wellbeing language and gives players, parents, schools and clubs an immediate point of recognition.

### 3. The homepage distinguishes Stump’d from two familiar extremes

“This isn’t sports psychology in a classroom. And it isn’t just a net session.” is useful positioning. It separates Stump’d from both a clinical/academic offer and ordinary technical cricket coaching.

### 4. It introduces repeatable tools rather than abstract promises

The emphasis on simple language and tools that can be used immediately is credible and aligned with Next Ball. This should be developed rather than replaced with broad claims about potential or transformation.

### 5. It contains the building blocks of a pathway

Foundation, Core and mentoring already suggest progression, and the listed delivery environments show that Stump’d is not tied to one venue or audience.

### 6. Founder credibility is human

The Rob and Jack section grounds Stump’d in lived cricket and delivery experience. Its tone—“built by people who’ve lived it”—fits the brand better than a corporate biography would.

### 7. Existing Wix functionality is useful

Wix Bookings, Members Area, Blog, Stores, Forms, Instagram Feed and Velo provide working infrastructure. The redesign should simplify what visitors see without casually deleting the systems behind it.

## What currently weakens the homepage

### 1. The opening positioning is too narrow

“Mental performance coaching for young cricketers” suggests an individual, performance-led coaching service. It does not quickly communicate schools, clubs, emotional awareness, confidence, communication or transfer beyond cricket.

The age claim “11–18” is also no longer correct for the full Stump’d pathway because Spark begins at ages 9–11 and Core is confirmed as ages 14–17.

### 2. The Stump’d 5 is represented incorrectly

The homepage says the Stump’d 5 help with “Awareness, Response, Intent, Reset, Next Ball”. Those may be useful concepts, but they are not the established Stump’d 5:

**Think. Reset. Focus. Communicate. Lead.**

This is a brand consistency issue, not merely a copy preference. The homepage must present the established five accurately. Next Ball should be introduced as a philosophy/tool flowing through the framework, not substituted for one of the five.

### 3. The pathway facts are out of date

- Spark is absent.
- Foundation is correctly 11–13 but should follow Spark.
- Core is shown as 13–18 rather than the confirmed 14–17.
- The current workshop durations should not be generalised across the redesigned site until each programme format is verified.
- Advanced 1-to-1 mentoring is presented as a third main level when it should sit beneath the primary Spark → Foundation → Core pathway.

### 4. Sections describe the organisation but do not route audiences

The homepage repeatedly explains the problem, framework and delivery model, yet a headteacher, club lead, parent or potential partner is not given a quick, explicit route tailored to their need. “View the pathway” is an internal concept, not an audience decision.

### 5. The delivery section mixes audiences and commercial models

“Clubs matter. Budgets are tight.” opens a homepage-wide section with a club-specific concern, then mixes club hosting, school delivery, parent payment, standalone workshops and ongoing programmes. This makes flexibility sound vague and risks creating questions about who pays before visitors understand the relevant offer.

Delivery details should live primarily on audience pages. The homepage only needs to show that Stump’d works with schools, clubs and individuals/parents and then route each audience appropriately.

### 6. The credibility section is thin and contains an error

The founder story is useful, but “30+ years playing experience” and “high-level club cricket” are broad claims without explaining why the team is suited to work with young people or organisations. The visible “IIf” typo damages care and credibility at the point where the page asks visitors to make contact.

This section should remain concise, link to About, and use only verified credentials and experience.

### 7. Competing and indirect calls to action

“Book a planning call” links to the general Book a Session page rather than the known planning-call booking URL. “View the pathway” appears in both the hero and final founder section. The repeated pair does not adapt to what the visitor has just learned.

Use one primary organisational CTA—**Book a planning call**—linked directly to the planning-call service. Use audience routes and contextual text links as secondary actions.

### 8. The Instagram feed is not a strong ending

Recent social content can prove that Stump’d is active, but an embedded feed should not become the final answer to “what do I do next?”. It can be reduced, moved below a strong closing CTA, or removed if it harms load time, mobile length or visual consistency.

### 9. The header is overloaded

The header currently asks visitors to process:

- 13 navigation destinations;
- five social networks;
- Summer Series twice;
- member login; and
- the site logo.

This is too much for a primary navigation, particularly on mobile. Summer Series has ended and must be removed. Social links belong in the footer. Log In should leave the header while the underlying inactive member area is preserved.

### 10. The footer is effectively missing

An empty footer removes the visitor’s safety net. It should provide concise navigation, contact/booking, social links and required policy links without duplicating the entire header.

## Page inventory findings

The 36-page list contains four different types of page and should not be treated as 36 equal navigation choices.

### Public proposition pages

- Homepage
- What We Do
- Our Approach
- Pathway
- Schools
- Clubs
- 1-to-1 Mentoring
- About
- Media & Partnerships
- FAQ’s
- Blog

Several of these may overlap. What We Do, Our Approach and Pathway should be reviewed together; their jobs must be distinct or they should be combined.

### Conversion and commerce pages

- Book a Session
- Shop
- Cart
- Intake Form
- Thank You

These are functional journey pages, not primary brand-navigation destinations. Preserve their Wix connections even if labels or entry points change.

### Captain’s Compass/member pages

- Captain’s Compass BETA Hub
- Start Captains Clarity
- My Captain’s Profile
- Plan My Game
- Match Day Mode
- FieldWise
- Review My Game
- Resources
- Parents/Coach Guide
- five player-type pages

The `/blank`, `/blank-1` … `/blank-12` URLs are poor public-facing names but may be tied to Velo, member navigation or app state. Do not rename, remove or redirect them until dependencies have been checked. They should not appear in the public primary navigation.

### Policy and trust pages

- Safeguarding
- Privacy Policy
- Terms & Conditions
- Refund Policy
- Accessibility Statement

These belong in the footer, with Safeguarding also available contextually where schools, clubs or parents need it.

### Outdated page

- Summer Series

Remove it from navigation and promotional areas. Before unpublishing or deleting the page, check traffic, inbound links, bookings, automations and SEO. If it is retired, redirect its URL to the most relevant current offer rather than creating a dead end.

## Proposed revised homepage flow

### 1. Hero — understand Stump’d

**Fixed H1:** Think Well. Play Better.

**Recommended proposition:** Mental strength through cricket—for sport, school and everyday life.

**Supporting line:** Cricket is the training ground. Confidence, focus and resilience are the skills they take with them.

Use one restrained primary action after the proposition. For a broad homepage, **See how Stump’d works** may be a better first action than forcing every visitor directly into a planning call. Keep **Book a planning call** visible but secondary until organisational visitors have context.

### 2. The moments that matter

Retain the concrete pressure/mistake material, but reduce it to one strong editorial section. Avoid framing children as a problem to be fixed.

Possible heading: **The game moves fast. The mind can learn to reset.**

Introduce FAST and CLEAR or Next Ball here, but not both at full length. Next Ball is the more universal homepage entry point.

### 3. Next Ball — the memorable idea

Explain in plain language:

- something happened;
- it cannot now be changed;
- the next response can be influenced.

Use **Step back. Take a breath. NEXT BALL.** as the memorable beat and show its relevance in cricket, school and everyday life.

### 4. The Stump’d 5 — the framework

Present the correct sequence once:

**Think. Reset. Focus. Communicate. Lead.**

Keep it visually strong and concise. Do not replace it with outcomes or overload it with explanatory cards.

### 5. Choose your route

Create three clear routes rather than a mixed delivery-model list:

- **Schools** — structured programmes and the Spark → Foundation → Core pathway.
- **Clubs** — practical mental-strength delivery in cricket environments.
- **Players & parents** — 1-to-1 mentoring and appropriate player support.

Media, foundations and commercial partners can use a quieter partnership route rather than competing equally in this section.

### 6. A pathway that grows with the player

Show a concise overview of Spark → Foundation → Core, followed by optional mentoring. Do not reproduce the full Schools pathway card copy on the homepage. Its purpose here is to prove structure and progression and link to the relevant detailed page.

### 7. Built from lived experience

Introduce Rob and Jack with one authentic photograph and a short, verified explanation of their cricket, delivery and youth-development experience. Link to About rather than placing full biographies on the homepage.

### 8. Evidence and activity

Use verified evidence only. The Gloucester Spark delivery is relevant, but the homepage should not become school-only. It can be one proof point alongside current activity or a restrained Instagram/news module.

### 9. Final action

**Heading:** Ready to start the conversation?

**Primary CTA:** Book a planning call

Link directly to:

`https://www.stumpd.co.uk/booking-calendar/planning-call?referral=service_list_widget`

Then provide the new footer as the recovery/navigation layer.

## Copy and terminology corrections

- Replace the broad homepage age claim `11–18`; the full pathway now begins at ages 9–11.
- Present the correct Stump’d 5.
- Add Spark before Foundation and Core.
- Change Core from `13–18` to `14–17`.
- Use `1-to-1`, not a mixture of `1-2-1`, `121` and `1-to-1`, unless a final brand convention says otherwise.
- Fix `IIf` immediately even before the wider redesign.
- Change `FAQ’s` to `FAQs` unless referring to something possessed by an FAQ.
- Remove Summer Series references.
- Link planning-call buttons directly to the planning-call booking service.
- Avoid implying that all offers share the same standalone/ongoing delivery model.

## Wix functionality protection

Before removing or renaming any page or visible element:

1. Check element connections, click actions and datasets in the Wix Editor.
2. Search Velo code for page IDs, element IDs, URLs and dataset references.
3. Check Wix Bookings, Forms, Stores and Members Area entry points.
4. Duplicate the current page before editing.
5. Preserve the existing slug until redirects and SEO implications are approved.
6. Test member-only Captain’s Compass journeys separately from public navigation.
7. Check whether the Instagram embed materially affects performance before retaining it.

Visual simplicity must not be achieved by silently breaking Wix functionality.

## Wix dependency report — confirmed

The connected Wix read-only inspection confirms:

- no homepage or site-wide Velo code is active;
- no homepage datasets or user-assigned IDs are referenced by code;
- the header and footer are global master-page components;
- the four homepage CTAs resolve only to `/book-a-session` and `/pathway`;
- Captain’s Compass pages remain protected by Wix Members authentication;
- removing the current Log In control would not delete or expose member content, but would remove the obvious sign-in route;
- the homepage Instagram module is the connected Wix Instagram Feed using `EXPAND` behaviour and Load more pagination; and
- What We Do, Our Approach and Pathway have no Velo dependencies, although all three remain in the global menu and Pathway receives two homepage links.

### Revised member-area decision

Do not simply remove member access. Replace the visually prominent generic Log In control with a quiet, explicit **Member log in** or **Captain’s Compass** utility link. Point it to the protected Captain’s Compass entry route so Wix can invoke authentication when required. Keep it visually secondary while the product remains in beta, but do not leave returning trial users without a discoverable route.

### Instagram decision

Retain the native Wix Instagram Feed for automatic updates during the first homepage build. Reduce its visual footprint and place it before the final CTA. Do not replace it with a manual gallery. Reassess only after measuring page performance and mobile length.

### Consolidation safety

What We Do and Our Approach have global-menu links but no discovered button, Velo or dataset dependency. Pathway has two homepage links but no Velo dependency. This makes consolidation technically plausible, but the pages should be redirected—not simply deleted—if their content and URLs are retired.

## SEO findings and approved actions

The current homepage has:

- title `Homepage | Stumpd`;
- no meta description;
- a canonical URL of `https://www.stumpd.co.uk`;
- a `noindex` directive;
- LocalBusiness and WebSite structured data; and
- a LocalBusiness address in Saint Mellons, Wales.

The `noindex` directive is the most urgent issue because it instructs search engines not to index the homepage. The owner has approved removing it so the redesigned public homepage can be indexed.

Recommended homepage metadata for approval:

- **SEO title:** `Stump’d | Mental Strength Through Cricket`
- **Meta description:** `Stump’d gives young people confidence, focus and resilience through cricket—skills they use on the pitch and apply to life off it.`

Retain the canonical homepage URL. The owner has confirmed that the Saint Mellons address is incorrect and that the registered Bristol address is a private home address which must not be published. Remove the incorrect postal address from the structured data and do not replace it with the home address. Review whether `Organization` plus `WebSite` structured data describes Stump’d more accurately than `LocalBusiness`; do not add a public street address.

3. Captain’s Compass remains quiet during beta, while its underlying member experience is preserved and prepared for a later public opening.
4. `What We Do`, `Our Approach` and `Pathway` may be consolidated where their content overlaps.
5. Retain a restrained Instagram feed if it remains useful and performs well; it is not mandatory and must not replace the final CTA.
6. Use existing Stump’d photography. Do not introduce stock or generated people.
