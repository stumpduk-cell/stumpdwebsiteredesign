# Stump’d Schools page — Phase 1 assessment

## Scope and evidence

This assessment covers the supplied master brief, the supplied desktop screenshots of the Schools page, and the supplied official logo PNG preview. It does **not** change the website or begin the Phase 3 build.

The live Wix URLs could not be retrieved from this environment: the web tool returned `401 Unauthorized` and a direct HTTPS request was blocked by the network proxy with `403`. Findings about page content and desktop presentation are therefore grounded in the screenshots; mobile findings are informed risks rather than claims from a live-device inspection. A live desktop, tablet and mobile review remains necessary before implementation.

## Executive assessment

The Schools page already has a recognisable Stump’d identity. Its dark field, gold editorial type, cream copy, real delivery photography and textured pathway graphic make it feel more like a confident sports campaign than a generic education or wellbeing site. That is its strongest asset and should be protected.

The main problem is not a lack of material; it is that the material does not yet form one clear decision journey. The editorial sections and the pathway graphic repeat benefits, delivery mechanics and reasons to choose Stump’d. The page consequently asks visitors to process the same proposition in several visual forms, while the two-stage pathway omits the newly defined entry point. The result has personality, but less clarity and momentum than it should.

The recommended direction is to retain the visual language, reduce repeated explanation, introduce Spark as the clear first step in a three-stage developmental pathway, and place one concise editorial Spark section immediately after that pathway. Evidence should follow Spark, practical logistics should be consolidated once, and a single strong planning-call action should close the journey.

## 1. What is working well

### A distinctive and appropriate visual identity

- The near-black background, gold/orange headings, cream copy and restrained red detailing are recognisably Stump’d and avoid both “clinical wellbeing” and generic school-site aesthetics.
- Oversized American Typewriter-style headings give the page an editorial, slightly rebellious voice. They carry the brand more effectively than a conventional sans-serif heading system would.
- The textured black/gold/red pathway artwork adds intensity and creates a useful contrast with the quieter editorial sections. The two modes belong together and should remain deliberately different.
- The official shield, brain and bat/lightning-bolt logo communicates the “thinking through cricket” proposition well. Its textured finish also explains and supports the campaign treatment. The supplied asset should remain the master; no recreation is required.

### Authenticity and useful restraint

- The wide school-hall photography is clearly rooted in real delivery. That credibility matters more here than a highly polished stock image.
- The page uses generous negative space and relatively short content blocks. This gives key phrases room to land and helps avoid the institutional tone common to school programme pages.
- Lines such as “Cricket has always trained hands. Stump’d trains minds.” are concise, memorable and make the distinction from ordinary coaching quickly.

### The beginnings of a clear offer

- “The Stump’d Pathway” is a strong organising idea. It suggests progression rather than a menu of unrelated workshops.
- Foundation and Core already distinguish younger introduction from older pressure/performance work.
- The “How It Works In Schools” checklist answers practical questions in a scannable format.
- The planning-call CTA is appropriately consultative for a school buyer and appears after explanatory content rather than before the proposition is understood.

## 2. What weakens the page

### Repetition without added meaning

The screenshots show several clusters covering substantially the same ground:

- programme outcomes such as emotional regulation, confidence after setbacks, responsibility and team behaviours;
- the pathway cards describing similar learning;
- “How It Works In Schools” explaining delivery;
- “Why Schools Work With Stump’d” repeating structured workshops, on-site delivery and timetable adaptation;
- a further “Structured Mental-Strength Pathways…” introduction after the graphic/CTA area.

Repetition can reinforce a message, but here it interrupts progression. Each section should have one job: establish the problem, define the offer, show progression, explain Spark, provide evidence, remove practical objections, or prompt action.

### An unclear programme hierarchy

- The current visual pathway begins with Foundation and Core, so it cannot represent the now-defined developmental journey.
- “Standalone Workshops Or Short Programmes” is broad and potentially misleading beside a precise Spark format. It should not describe Spark.
- Optional 1-to-1 mentoring is given substantial space within the pathway graphic. It is relevant, but it reads like a near-equal pathway stage rather than secondary support for older students.
- The page appears to move from pathway to CTA and then back into more pathway positioning. This weakens the sense that the visitor is moving towards a decision.

### Hierarchy and readability issues

- Some gold editorial headings are so large that they approach or exceed the useful content width. “Why Schools Work With Stump’d.” and “Structured Mental-Strength Pathways…” become visual events but are harder to scan and vulnerable to poor wrapping.
- Cream body copy appears fine at larger sizes, but smaller thin type over black/textured areas risks insufficient practical readability, especially on lower-brightness screens.
- The blue tick emoji conflicts with the core palette and introduces a generic interface style. A simple gold or cream tick/bullet treatment would be more coherent and easier to control across devices.
- Several sections have large empty areas while the pathway graphic itself is information-dense. The page therefore alternates between too sparse and too compressed rather than using one consistent spacing rhythm.
- The pathway artwork contains small copy baked into a large composition. It may be legible on a wide desktop, but scaling the whole graphic down is unlikely to work on mobile and may create accessibility problems because its text is not responsive or selectable.

### Navigation and action distractions

- The screenshot shows prominent social icons, “Log In”, and a “Summer Series” button above the Schools content. For a school decision-maker, these elements compete with the page task before the proposition begins.
- “Summer Series” appears seasonal and player-facing. Unless it is current and strategically important, it should not hold the most prominent navigation treatment on the Schools journey.
- The page would benefit from one primary CTA label used consistently. “Book a planning call” is the strongest candidate; “get in touch” can remain a lower-emphasis text alternative where needed.

## 3. What to keep

Keep and refine rather than replace:

1. The core palette: `#10141e`, `#cf7f20`, `#a21340`, and `#decdaf`.
2. American Typewriter for editorial headings and Roboto for functional/body copy, subject to a real-device size and contrast check.
3. The official logo at its original proportions, with enough clear space and no substitute mark.
4. The dark editorial sections with oversized gold headings, cream supporting copy and purposeful breathing room.
5. The textured campaign treatment specifically for the pathway and occasional emphasis—not for every section.
6. Authentic delivery photography, especially images that show interaction, thought, communication and movement.
7. “The Stump’d Pathway” as the organising model.
8. Concise, practical lists where they answer buyer questions.
9. A planning-call CTA as the primary conversion action.
10. The contrast between expressive campaign moments and calm editorial explanation.

## 4. What to remove, combine or restructure

### Remove or demote

- Remove “Standalone Workshops Or Short Programmes” as a blanket delivery statement. Replace it with programme-specific facts, beginning with Spark’s exact two-session model.
- Remove the duplicated “Why Schools Work With Stump’d” checklist if its points are already covered in the consolidated delivery section.
- Remove or rewrite the second pathway-style introduction if it repeats the proposition after the pathway graphic.
- Demote 1-to-1 mentoring to a short secondary strip or note after Core. It should not interrupt the three main stages.
- Remove prominent Summer Series navigation from the Schools context if the offer is outdated; if it remains current, place it in audience-appropriate navigation rather than as the dominant header action.
- Avoid decorative blue emoji ticks. Use accessible HTML lists with brand-coloured markers.

### Combine

- Combine all programme-wide outcomes into the opening “why this matters / what Stump’d does” section. Keep it short and do not repeat the full list later.
- Combine “How It Works” and the practical reasons schools choose Stump’d into one logistics section: on-site delivery, spaces, equipment, staff presence, timetable fit and scalable group numbers.
- Keep Spark-specific format facts only in the Spark section and, in abbreviated form, on its pathway card.
- Pair the Gloucester proof point directly with Spark. It validates the programme and the Next Ball language at the moment the visitor is evaluating Spark.

### Restructure

- Make each section answer a distinct visitor question.
- Place the pathway before detailed programme content so visitors first see the whole developmental model.
- Follow the pathway immediately with Spark because it is the priority and the entry stage—not because it is a separate campaign bolted onto the page.
- Delay the main CTA until after evidence and logistics. A quiet text link after Spark is acceptable, but repeated full-width buttons would dilute impact.

## 5. Where Spark should sit

Spark should appear in two connected places:

1. **First in the pathway**, before Foundation and Core, so the progression is understood at a glance.
2. **Immediately after the pathway as a concise editorial section**, because it is the immediate priority and needs enough detail to answer school buyers’ questions.

This ordering creates a useful reveal: visitors first understand that Stump’d supports development over time, then explore the first stage in practical detail. Spark should not sit at the bottom of the current page and should not become an oversized landing page embedded inside it.

## 6. Pathway recommendation: Spark → Foundation → Core

### The progression to communicate

- **Spark — Years 5–6 / ages 9–11:** recognise emotions, build confidence after mistakes, communicate and learn simple reset tools before pressure increases.
- **Foundation — ages 11–13:** retain the current introductory mental-strength role, refined only enough to avoid overlap with Spark.
- **Core — ages 14–17:** retain the current focus on pressure, performance and responsibility.

The connecting idea should be increasing age, understanding and pressure. Use a clear line or directional progression, but do not rely on arrows alone; labels and document order must preserve meaning for screen readers and mobile users.

### Spark card content hierarchy

The Spark card should match Foundation and Core in dimensions, border language, texture and typographic hierarchy. Recommended content density:

- **SPARK**
- **Years 5–6 | Ages 9–11**
- Confidence • Emotions • Teamwork
- Building confidence before pressure increases.
- A maximum of three short learning points, not a paragraph.
- **Think. Reset. Next Ball.**

The exact two-session delivery format belongs in the dedicated section rather than being squeezed into the card.

### Responsive construction

The pathway should be rebuilt as real Wix layout elements/cards rather than one desktop graphic containing all text. On desktop, present three equal connected cards in a horizontal sequence. On tablet, either preserve three columns only if the copy remains comfortably readable or use a two-plus-one layout with an unambiguous connector. On mobile, stack Spark, Foundation and Core vertically in document order with a vertical progression line. Texture may sit behind the cards, but text must remain live, selectable and responsive.

The optional 1-to-1 mentoring offer should become a slim, lower-contrast supporting row beneath Core, labelled clearly as optional and for ages 13+.

## 7. Dedicated Spark section

The Spark section should use the quieter editorial language, preventing the page from becoming a continuous sequence of dense campaign cards.

### Recommended visual composition

- Black/navy background with one oversized gold **“Spark.”** heading.
- A cream subheading: **“Mental strength starts before the pressure does.”**
- A two-column desktop composition: concise copy and practical facts on one side; one strong authentic photograph on the other.
- The photograph should show real participation or interaction rather than a distant empty facility. Crop around faces, gestures or team communication while retaining appropriate consent and safeguarding considerations.
- A restrained magenta accent can mark the Next Ball moment or a rule, but should not become a separate visual theme.
- On mobile, use the order: heading → proposition → photograph → practical facts → Next Ball statement. This prevents the image from separating the heading from its meaning.

### Content hierarchy

1. `Spark.`
2. `Mental strength starts before the pressure does.`
3. Two short sentences defining Spark for Years 5 and 6.
4. A deliberate Next Ball statement: **“You can’t change what just happened. You can choose what you do next.”**
5. One scan-friendly factual list:
   - Years 5 & 6 | Ages 9–11
   - Maximum 12 pupils per group
   - Two 2-hour sessions per group
   - Sessions one week apart
   - As many groups as the school requires
6. **“Step back. Take a breath. NEXT BALL.”** as the closing beat.

Equipment, available spaces and staff presence should be held for the consolidated logistics section, avoiding an overlong Spark block.

## 8. Desktop, tablet and mobile issues

### Desktop observations

- Very large headings create personality but some extend beyond an easy reading width and appear vulnerable to clipping or awkward line breaks.
- Content alignment varies between central graphic sections and left-aligned editorial sections without always feeling intentional.
- Wide empty regions make the page longer without necessarily improving comprehension.
- The pathway graphic is visually strong but too copy-dense relative to the surrounding page.
- The social/login/Summer Series utility row competes with the Schools message.
- Some authentic photographs are cropped very wide and distant, showing the environment more than the children or interaction.

### Mobile risks requiring live verification

- Fixed pixel heading sizes may produce one-word-per-line wrapping or horizontal overflow. Use Wix responsive type scales with a sensible minimum and maximum rather than merely shrinking the desktop composition.
- A single-image pathway will become unreadable when scaled. Rebuild it as responsive cards.
- Long checklists can become scroll-heavy; group closely related facts and keep each line concise.
- Image focal points may be lost in automatic centre crops. Set mobile-specific crops/focal points.
- Touch targets in navigation and CTA areas should be at least comfortably thumb-sized and separated.
- Cream text using thin type may lose clarity on small screens; body copy should use Roboto at a practical size, weight and line height.
- Excess vertical spacing that feels editorial on desktop may become empty scrolling on mobile. Use a tighter but consistent mobile spacing scale.
- The navigation needs a mobile task test: users should be able to identify Schools and reach the planning call without opening several ambiguous layers.

### Accessibility checks for Phase 4

- Test actual foreground/background pairs to WCAG AA rather than assuming palette colours pass at every size and weight.
- Keep headings as semantic text, not text embedded in images.
- Add meaningful alternative text to delivery photography; decorative texture should have no announced description.
- Do not use colour or connecting lines as the only indication of pathway order.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences if pathway or text animation is introduced.
- Check keyboard focus visibility and Wix form/booking accessibility.

## 9. Proposed revised page flow

### 1. School-specific opening: what this is

One clear proposition for a school audience:

- Stump’d teaches mental strength through cricket.
- Cricket is the training ground; confidence, focus and resilience transfer to sport, school and everyday life.
- Retain **“Think Well. Play Better.”** as the fixed brand line.

Use one authentic hero image and avoid placing several CTAs before the offer is clear.

### 2. Why it matters: what changes for young people

A concise editorial section linking mistakes, pressure and emotions to practical responses. Introduce the Stump’d distinction and the Stump’d 5 once. Do not reproduce a long generic benefits list.

### 3. The Stump’d Pathway

Present **Spark → Foundation → Core** as one responsive developmental system. Keep the campaign aesthetic and make progression explicit. Place optional mentoring beneath it as secondary support.

### 4. Spark

Use the dedicated editorial composition described above. Keep Next Ball central and the delivery facts exact.

### 5. Already in schools

Use a compact proof section:

- “Delivered for Gloucester Rugby Foundation using BBC Children in Need funding.”
- Multiple schools across the GL postcode.
- More than 50 children took part across the term.
- Each group still received two sessions, one week apart; the wider delivery period reflected multiple groups.
- Children and teachers began using “Next Ball” themselves beyond the immediate exercises.

Frame this as observed adoption, not as a testimonial or unsupported impact claim. “More importantly, Next Ball stuck.” is a strong editorial bridge if the owner is happy with it.

### 6. How it works in your school

Consolidate all practical information here:

- Stump’d supplies the equipment.
- The school supplies an appropriate classroom, hall or outdoor space; no specialist cricket facility is required.
- School staff remain present.
- Delivery can fit the school timetable and scale to as many groups as required.
- Programme-specific formats remain clearly labelled rather than described collectively as “short programmes”.

### 7. Next action

Close with a short invitation and one primary **“Book a planning call”** button linking to the existing Wix booking page. A lower-emphasis contact route can sit beneath it for visitors not ready to book.

This sequence answers, in order: What is Stump’d? Why does it matter? What is available? Where does Spark fit? Has it been delivered? Can it work in my school? What do I do next?
